# Hospital appointment booking UC-01

## Brief

UC-01 Intelligent Appointment Booking (university project). Four agents: Agent 0 Coordinator (encloses the whole workflow), Agent A Chat agent, Agent B Classifier (System One model, Laya), Agent C Booker. All agents are treated as external: none of them receives direct patient identifiers.

TRIGGER: the hospital application (patient logged in) posts the request to a webhook. Start data: request_id; patient_id, patient_name, fiscal_code, email, phone, date_of_birth (PERSONAL DATA, direct identifiers); message (PERSONAL DATA: free text, may contain health information), e.g. "I need a cardiology appointment, preferably next week in the morning"; registered, referral_required, referral_present (administrative facts from the Patient Database, synthetic demo data).

STEPS
1. Pseudonymise patient data (software, bpmn8n on this server, privacy gate): replaces patient_id with a stable pseudonym patient_ref (HMAC-SHA256 with the server's SECRET_KEY) and redacts identifiers from the text: the patient's own name, fiscal code, email, phone and date of birth from the start data, plus patterns for emails, phone numbers, Italian codice fiscale and dates of birth. It keeps the redacted conversation (first message, then every agent question and patient answer). It runs after the start and after every follow-up answer.
2. AI Agent collects patient information and preferences (Agent A, LLM agent, external provider Gemini): reads only the redacted conversation and today's date; proposes the specialty (service), first and last acceptable dates, time of day (morning, afternoon, any), preferred doctor, and one follow-up question for what is missing. It never asks for identity data (known from the login) and never infers a specialty from symptoms.
3. Read booking preferences (software): reads Agent A's answer into fields and decides completeness: service, valid ISO dates (first <= last) and time of day must all be present.
4. Information complete? (rule on the data): No -> Ask patient for missing information (patient, patients lane: answers Agent A's question, shown as written by an AI model) -> Pseudonymise patient data -> Agent A again. Yes -> step 5.
5. Validate request and hospital requirements (software, business rules, internal bpmn8n step): registered? referral required and present? service bookable (in the service directory)? dates valid and not in the past? Produces a concise validation report WITHOUT identifiers and the first failed check (none, registration, referral, service, dates).
6. Request valid? (Agent B, Laya on this server): reads only the validation report. Yes -> step 7. No, or unsure (below 0.7 confidence) -> Escalate to hospital staff.
7. Escalate to hospital staff (Agent 0 Coordinator, software with process mining): from bpmn8n's event log and open user tasks it computes the staff queues (open tasks and oldest waiting time per lane, average handling time over 30 days) and how many follow-up questions this case needed; it picks the desk (registration/referral problems: admissions desk; otherwise booking desk, synthetic demo desks), the priority (high when the patient already answered 3 or more follow-up questions or all checks passed but the classifier was unsure; else normal) and advice when a queue needs more people. It sees only the pseudonym and the redacted report. Then Request closed or referred to staff (person, hospital-staff lane: closes the request or takes it over, notes the outcome; staff may see the patient's real name and contacts to call back). End.
8. Search doctors and available slots (Agent C, software on the hospital scheduling database, synthetic demo slots): free future slots filtered by specialty, dates, time of day and preferred doctor; if none, alternatives (dates up to 14 days around the request, another time of day, other doctors of the same specialty). Uses no identifiers.
9. Slots available? (rule): Yes -> Present appointment options (Agent C, notifies the patient app with the numbered options) -> Patient selects appointment (patient, patients lane, sees the options and Agent A's reading of the request) -> Booking service rechecks and reserves slot (Agent C, rechecks the slot is still free and reserves it once per request under the pseudonym patient_ref) -> Booking successful? (rule): Yes -> Send confirmation to patient (internal bpmn8n step, email with date, time, doctor and location; uses the real email and name) -> Appointment confirmed (patient acknowledges, patients lane). End. No (slot taken meanwhile) -> back to Search doctors and available slots with updated options.
   No -> Suggest alternative dates or doctors (patient, patients lane: sees the alternatives, gives new preferences or says no option suits) -> New preference: back to Search doctors and available slots; No suitable option: Request closed or referred to staff. End.

DECISIONS AND ACCOUNTABILITY: completeness and booking outcome are rules on the data; validity is Agent B on the redacted report, with staff deciding every rejection or unclear case; the patient chooses the slot, the new preferences and whether to give up, and acknowledges the confirmation; staff close or take over escalated and abandoned requests.

EXCEPTIONS: missing information loops through the patient; invalid or unclear requests and abandoned requests go to staff; no matching slot leads to alternatives; a slot taken between search and reservation leads to a new search. Not specified (synthetic demo data): the hospital's real directory, referral rules, desks, notification channels, reminders and timeouts.

## Who decides

**LLM agent**: One llm_agent: Agent A, the chat agent, because understanding free-text requests with relative dates and writing a natural follow-up question is open-ended language work no rule or fixed set of branches does well. Everything else is not LLM work: completeness, validation, slot search, booking and escalation are rules and database queries; validity is routed by Laya (on this server); field reading after the agent is deterministic parsing of its fixed-format answer. The provider (Gemini) is external, so the agent receives only the redacted conversation and today's date, never an identifier, and never decides: its fields are checked by rules and the patient answers its questions and chooses the appointment.

**GDPR**: Personal data: patient_id, patient_name, fiscal_code, email, phone, date_of_birth (direct identifiers, from the hospital app's login, needed to know who books and to confirm); the request text (may contain health information: the specialty; needed to book); registered/referral facts (Patient Database, needed for the hospital's rules). Anonymisation: all four agents are treated as external and none receives a direct identifier. 'Pseudonymise patient data' runs on this server after the start and after every follow-up answer: it replaces the patient id with patient_ref (HMAC-SHA256 keyed with the server's SECRET_KEY, stable and not reversible without the key) and redacts the patient's own name, fiscal code, email, phone and date of birth plus email, phone, codice fiscale and date-of-birth patterns from the text. Agent A (Gemini, external) sees only {{request_date}} and {{redacted_conversation}}: the specialty and preferences stay in it (pseudonymised data, the minimum the work needs; a real deployment needs a processing agreement with the provider or a local model). Agent B (Laya) reads only {{validation_report}}, which holds no identifiers. Agent C (search, present, reserve) gets service, dates, time of day, doctor, slot ids and request_id; the reservation is stored under patient_ref (fields patient_id = {{patient_ref}}). Agent 0 (coordinator) gets patient_ref, failed_check and the redacted report, plus aggregate queue figures. Only bpmn8n's internal steps use real identifiers: the pseudonymisation itself, and sending the confirmation (email and name); hospital staff see name and contacts in their task to call the patient back. People decide: the patient answers every question, chooses the slot, changes preferences or gives up, and acknowledges the confirmation; staff decide every invalid, unclear or abandoned request. Explanation afterwards: bpmn8n's event log keeps every step's input and result, Agent A's prompt and answer, Laya's state, branch and confidence, and the coordinator's figures.

- **Pseudonymise patient data** (software): a mechanical privacy rule (HMAC pseudonym, known identifiers and regex patterns) that must run on this server before any agent reads the text
- **AI Agent collects patient information and preferences** (llm_agent): Agent A: open-ended natural-language understanding (relative dates like 'next week', vague wording) and a friendly follow-up question; it only proposes fields from redacted text, rules check them and the patient answers and chooses
- **Information complete?** (condition_gateway): an explicit rule: specialty, valid dates and time of day present
- **Validate request and hospital requirements** (software): the hospital's rules (registration, referral, bookable service, dates) are known and mechanical
- **Request valid?** (gateway): Agent B, the System One classifier (Laya, on this server), matches the redacted validation report to yes or no as the project requires; below 0.7 confidence its default goes to staff, and every no is decided by staff
- **Escalate to hospital staff** (software): Agent 0, the coordinator: desk, priority and staffing advice follow from explicit rules over process-mining figures of bpmn8n's own event log; staff decide
- **Request closed or referred to staff** (person): closing or taking over a patient's request needs accountability and possibly a call to the patient
- **Slots available?** (condition_gateway): a rule on the search result
- **Patient selects appointment** (person): only the patient chooses the appointment
- **Booking successful?** (condition_gateway): a rule on the reservation result; the patient chose the slot and acknowledges the confirmation
- **Suggest alternative dates or doctors** (person): the patient decides whether to change preferences or give up
- **Appointment confirmed** (person): the patient acknowledges the confirmed appointment, closing the case

## Interview

**user**: Patients book a hospital appointment by describing it in natural language, for example "I need a cardiology appointment, preferably next week in the morning". An AI chat agent understands the request and asks follow-up questions until the patient's identity, specialty, preferred dates and times and administrative details are complete. The request is validated against the hospital's rules (registered patient, referral, bookable service); invalid or unclear requests go to hospital staff. A booking agent searches doctors and free slots, presents the options or suggests alternatives, rechecks and reserves the slot the patient picks, and sends a confirmation with date, time, doctor and location. A coordinator watches every case and uses process mining on the event log to route escalations to the least loaded staff team. Patient identifiers are pseudonymised before any agent sees the request.

**assistant**: {"question": "What starts a booking case, and with which data? For example: does the hospital app send the patient's free-text request together with who the patient is (id, name, contact details) and what the hospital already knows about them?"}

**user**: The patient, logged into the hospital application, writes the request in natural language, e.g. "I need a cardiology appointment, preferably next week in the morning". The app starts the case with a request id, the patient's id, name, fiscal code, email, phone and date of birth, the message, and the administrative facts it already holds from the Patient Database: whether the patient is registered, whether a referral is required and whether one is on file. In this university project those facts are synthetic demo data.

**assistant**: {"question": "Who reads the request and collects what is still missing, and which details must be complete before the booking can go on?"}

**user**: Agent A, the chat agent, understands the natural-language context. It must collect the medical specialty, the preferred dates and the preferred time of day (morning, afternoon or any), and a preferred doctor if the patient has one. The patient's identity is already known from the login, so the agent never asks for it. If something is missing the agent asks the patient a follow-up question and reads the answer, until the request is complete. Agent A is an external LLM (the configured provider is Gemini).

**assistant**: {"question": "Since Agent A is an external LLM, which personal data may it see?"}

**user**: No direct identifiers. All the agents (A, B, C and the coordinator) are in theory external, so before Agent A reads anything the patient's text goes through a pseudonymisation step on our server: the patient id becomes a stable pseudonym (an HMAC with the server's secret key) and names, fiscal codes, emails, phone numbers and dates of birth are redacted from the text. This happens after the first request and after every follow-up answer. Only bpmn8n's own internal steps (validating the request, sending the confirmation) and hospital staff use the real identifiers.

**assistant**: {"question": "How is a complete request validated, who decides when it is not valid, and what happens to unclear cases?"}

**user**: The system checks the hospital's rules: the patient is registered, a referral is present when one is required, the requested service can be booked (it is in the hospital's service directory) and the dates are valid. Agent B, the classifier (the System One model, Laya, on our server), takes that schema and matches the data: it reads only a short validation report without identifiers and says yes or no. When it says no, or is unsure, the request is escalated to hospital staff, who close it or take it over.

**assistant**: {"question": "How are the staff chosen for an escalation, and who watches the cases?"}

**user**: Agent 0, the coordinator, encloses the whole workflow: it watches the state of each case and whether the patient managed to complete the request, and contacts staff when needed. It has process mining tools on bpmn8n's event log and open tasks (queue length and waiting times per lane, how many follow-up questions the case needed) so the hospital can allocate its people dynamically: it picks the staff desk and the priority of each escalation and says when a queue needs more people. It works on the pseudonym only. Staff work in the hospital-staff lane; how many desks the hospital has is not specified, use a registration/referral desk and a booking desk as synthetic demo data.

**assistant**: {"question": "Once the request is valid, how are doctors and slots found and offered, and what if nothing matches?"}

**user**: Agent C, the booker, has access to the hospital scheduling database. It searches doctors and free slots filtered by specialty, dates, time of day and preferred doctor. If there are slots it presents them and the patient selects one. If there are none it suggests alternatives (wider dates, another time of day, other compatible doctors); the patient can give new preferences and search again, or say that no option suits, and then the request is closed or referred to staff. The scheduling system is not specified: use the synthetic demo slots already in the hospital project (two cardiology slots, 10 November 2026 at 09:00 and 11 November 2026 at 14:00).

**assistant**: {"question": "What happens when the patient picks a slot, and how is the booking confirmed?"}

**user**: The booking service rechecks that the slot is still free and reserves it, under the patient's pseudonym. If the slot has meanwhile been taken, Agent C searches again and offers updated options. After a successful booking the hospital database holds it and the system sends the patient a confirmation with date, time, doctor and location to the patient's email; the patient then sees the confirmed appointment in the app. Real notification channels are not specified: the demo uses the server's mail backend.

**assistant**: {"question": "When does a case end, and who is accountable at each end?"}

**user**: The workflow ends when the appointment is confirmed (the patient acknowledges it in the patients lane), when the patient abandons the request, or when it is transferred to hospital staff; in the last two cases a staff member in the hospital-staff lane closes the request or takes it over and notes the outcome. Waiting times and reminders are not specified.
